\begin{abstract}
Layer normalization constrains transformer hidden states to a sphere whose
radius is a measured quantity, $\|\gamma_l\|$ per layer (not the textbook
$\sqrt{d}$). Within this sphere, a single tangent step toward any token's LM
head direction reaches that token with 91--98\% reliability across four model
families and never lowers its rank (100\% improvement). We propose a theory of edge-of-chaos dynamics: the per-model Lyapunov
rate clusters near $\lambda \cdot L_{\mathrm{comp}} \approx 0.5$ for the Gemma,
GPT-2, and mid-Qwen families (spanning 6 of 13 tested architectures). The BOS
token defines a growing reference axis orthogonal to vocabulary tokens across
28 layers. Some tokens are self-reinforcing fixed points (cow tipping)---the
degenerate limit of the dynamics at $\lambda \to 0$---with narrow basins that
can be encoded into data as a defense against LLM scraping. All measurements on
Qwen2.5-7B with spot-checks on DeepSeek-7B and Mistral-7B. Full reproduction
steps provided. Cow tipping occurs on all tested models, with
vocabulary-specific pit tokens.
\end{abstract}

\section{The Sphere: A New Measurement}

RMSNorm maps hidden states to a sphere whose expected radius is $\|\gamma_l\|$:
\[
\|\operatorname{RMSNorm}(h)\|^2 = \sum_{i=1}^d \gamma_{l,i}^2 \left(\frac{h_i}{\operatorname{RMS}(h)}\right)^2
\]

Per-instance norms float around this value (ratio 0.7--1.3), so the picture is a
fuzzy shell. Measured $\gamma$ norms across four model families:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
\toprule
Model & $d$ & $\|\gamma_0\|$ & $\|\gamma_{\mathrm{mid}}\|$ & $\|\gamma_{\mathrm{end}}\|$ \\
\midrule
Mistral-7B & 4096 & 16.6 & 111--158 & 195.5 \\
DeepSeek-7B & 4096 & 3.7 & 15--24 & 26.1 \\
Qwen2-1.5B & 1536 & 20.0 & 40--60 & 71.0 \\
Qwen2.5-7B & 3584 & 17.0 & 63.5 & 90.0 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

$\|\gamma_l\|$ is learned, varying across architectures even at the same~$d$.

\section{BOS Axis}

The BOS token (position~0) defines a reference direction $\hat{b}_l =
h_l^{(0)}/\|h_l^{(0)}\|$. Its residual norm grows from 2.8 to 102.2 across
28 layers, consistent with attention-sinking~\cite{xiao2023efficient}. LM head
rows $W_t$ are approximately orthogonal to BOS at every layer ($\theta_{l,t} =
\arccos|\hat{b}_l \cdot \hat{w}_t|$):

\small
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Layer & $\|\operatorname{LN}(h)\|$ & $\|\mathrm{BOS}\|$ & Token--BOS angle \\
\midrule
0  & 15.9 & 2.8  & 88--90$^\circ$ \\
2  & 16.3 & 24.8 & 88--90$^\circ$ \\
4  & 33.5 & 21.8 & 89--90$^\circ$ \\
6  & 31.8 & 36.9 & 89--90$^\circ$ \\
8  & 36.5 & 44.1 & 88--90$^\circ$ \\
10 & 34.7 & 43.4 & 89--90$^\circ$ \\
12 & 37.5 & 50.3 & 89--90$^\circ$ \\
14 & 42.9 & 55.2 & 88--90$^\circ$ \\
16 & 44.4 & 53.0 & 89--90$^\circ$ \\
18 & 40.7 & 43.9 & 89--90$^\circ$ \\
20 & 45.2 & 48.2 & 89--90$^\circ$ \\
22 & 54.9 & 63.7 & 89--89$^\circ$ \\
24 & 55.5 & 68.7 & 87--90$^\circ$ \\

\end{center}
\normalsize

High-dimensional vectors are generically near-orthogonal ($\mathbb{E}[|\cos|]
\approx 1/\sqrt{d}$), so the approximate $90^\circ$ is expected; the consistent
preservation of this angle while BOS grows $36\times$ across layers is the
observed regularity. Mistral-7B and DeepSeek-7B also give 87--90$^\circ$.

\section{Sphere Steering: Tangent Traversal}

The LM head row $W_t$ has a tangent component $g_t = W_t - (W_t \cdot
\hat{h})\hat{h}$. Moving $h$ along $g_t$ increases the target logit ($W_t \cdot
g_t = \|g_t\|^2 \geq 0$). Renormalizing keeps the traversal on the sphere:
\[
h' = \frac{h + \alpha g_t}{\|h + \alpha g_t\|} \cdot \|\gamma_L\|
\]

\subsection{Quantitative Traversal}

The LM head itself provides a complete geometric map of the output space:
152,064 directions, one per token, on the sphere. A single tangent step at
$\alpha=0.3$ reaches any of them. Tested on 1,000 random tokens across four
model families:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
Model & Rank 1 & Rank improved \\
\midrule
Qwen2.5-7B & 94.5\% & 100.0\% \\
DeepSeek-7B & 97.5\% & 100.0\% \\
Mistral-7B & 96.2\% & 100.0\% \\
Qwen2-1.5B & 91.3\% & 100.0\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The rank improvement is guaranteed ($W_t \cdot g_t = \|g_t\|^2 \geq 0$), and the
91--98\% reaching rank 1 follows from the fact that most LM head rows are
isolated enough from their nearest neighbors to dominate the logit after a
single tangent step. The fraction that fail are tokens in clusters (digits,
question marks) where nearest-neighbor LM head rows compete.

\subsection{Qualitative Demonstration}

On Qwen2.5-7B, steering open-ended prompts produces visible output shifts. At
$\alpha=2$ on ``Tell me something interesting:'', steering toward ``75'' changes
the continuation from a generic fact (``The most common blood type is O
positive...'') to a numeric statement (``1984 was the last year the United
States had a budget surplus...''). At $\alpha=3$ on ``Once upon a time'',
steering toward ``apple'' turns the story from ``a young boy named Timmy...''
into ``apple trees were planted in the garden of Eden...'' Common words and
digits shift more readily than rare tokens; larger $\alpha$ trades fluency for
strength.

Steering does not distinguish censored from uncensored paths. It is a
white-box traversal primitive: with access to the model weights, anyone can move
$h$ toward any token direction on the sphere without retraining or fine-tuning.

\section{Cow Tipping}

Some tokens generate themselves when fed repeatedly. A token~$T$ is
\emph{self-consistent} if $s(T) = \operatorname{softmax}(W \cdot h_T)[T]$ is
high, where $h_T = \operatorname{model}(e_T)$. A \emph{pit} satisfies both
high~$s(T)$ and trajectory stability $\cos(h_{n+1}, h_n) \approx 1$:
indefinite self-generation.

We scanned a subset of the Qwen2.5-7B vocabulary, testing permanence (3 input
repetitions, 15-step self-prediction). Seven tokens pass:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Token & ID & $s(T)$ & Trigger \\
\midrule
$\mathtt{0}$ & 15 & 0.975 & $\mathtt{000}$, $\mathtt{000-000-0000}$ \\
$\mathtt{ab}$ & 370 & 0.934 & $\mathtt{ababab}$ \\
$\mathtt{.}$ (space+dot) & 659 & 0.889 & $\mathtt{.\ .\ .}$ \\
$\mathtt{ere}$ & 485 & 0.878 & $\mathtt{erere}$ \\
$\mathtt{\_}$ (space+underscore) & 716 & 0.110 & $\mathtt{\_\ \_}$ (3+ reps) \\
NULL byte & 188 & 0.978 & 3+ consecutive null bytes \\
$\mathtt{????}$ & 26610 & 0.386$^\dag$ & 8+ question marks \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

$^\dag$Deterministic fixed point ($\cos \approx 1.0$ once generated).

The $\mathtt{0}$ token is clearest: $\mathtt{000}$ yields $p = 0.974$, $\cos
\approx 1.0$, $p > 0.95$ within 3 steps. Phone numbers trigger it via three
consecutive token-15 units. The $\mathtt{0}$ pit was confirmed on DeepSeek-7B.
Mistral-7B has its own set of self-consistent tokens (e.g., its vocabulary
contains different pits with $s$ up to 0.59 and 15/15 permanence), confirming
that the phenomenon is general but the specific pit tokens are
vocabulary-dependent. All pits have narrow basins---any trailing token disrupts
the fixed point.

\subsection{Defensive Encoding}

The pits can be used as a defensive primitive: embedding pit triggers into data
causes any model that processes it to fall into a fixed-point loop rather than
continue reading. Because the basins are narrow, the trigger must be the
terminal content of each chunk. We verified three encodings on Qwen2.5-7B:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{NULL bytes.} Appending $\mathtt{\backslash x00\backslash x00\backslash x00}$ to HTML makes the model emit null bytes indefinitely. The trigger is invisible in rendered text ($s = 0.978$).
\item \textbf{Repeated sub-tokens.} A chunk ending in $\mathtt{cut\ cut\ cut}$ collapses the model into $\mathtt{cut\ cut\ cut\ldots}$; $\mathtt{key}$ requires 6 repetitions to lock.
\item \textbf{Phone number (terminal).} $\mathtt{000-000-0000}$ locks only as the final content; any trailing markup breaks it.
\end{itemize}

A crawler reading a page that terminates in one of these triggers stops
producing useful output and repeats the pit token instead. We report this as a
defensive capability: it lets a content owner degrade automated scraping by
large language models without affecting human readers.

\section{Edge of Chaos: A Theory}

The residual stream is a dynamical system $h_{l+1} = h_l + f_l(h_l)$. Its
per-layer Lyapunov exponent measures how perturbations grow:

\[
\lambda_l = \ln\frac{\|h_{l+1}^{\mathrm{pert}} - h_{l+1}^{\mathrm{ref}}\|}{\|h_l^{\mathrm{pert}} - h_l^{\mathrm{ref}}\|}
\]

A perturbation that neither explodes nor vanishes during computation would place
the system at the edge of chaos, corresponding to $\lambda \cdot
L_{\mathrm{comp}} \approx 0.5$ (one e-fold of total expansion). We measured the
per-layer rate across 13 model families:

\small
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llc}
\toprule
Cluster & Models & $\lambda \cdot L_{\mathrm{comp}}$ \\
\midrule
$\approx 0.5$ & Gemma-2-2B, Gemma-2-9B, GPT-2, DS-R1-7B, Qwen-1.5B, Qwen2.5-7B & 0.37--0.57 \\
$\approx 0.8$ & SmolLM2-360M, DeepSeek-7B & 0.76--0.88 \\
$\approx 1.0$ & Phi-3.5-mini, Mistral-7B & 1.00--1.47 \\
$\approx 0.1$ & Qwen2.5-0.5B, Qwen2.5-3B & 0.01--0.10 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\normalsize

The edge-of-chaos balance ($\lambda \cdot L \approx 0.5$) holds for roughly
half the models---notably the Gemma family, GPT-2, and mid-sized Qwen---but is
not universal. We present it as a theory that captures a subset of
architectures, not as a scaling law. Cow tipping (Section~4) is the degenerate
limit of this balance: a fixed point where $\lambda_l \to 0$ and the trajectory
stops changing entirely.

\section{Reproduction}

All measurements are scriptable in under 100 lines of Python. Full code at
\url{https://github.com/ntrillard/transformer-geometry}. The procedure:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Sphere radius.} Extract $\gamma_l$ from model state dict. Feed
prompts, record post-LN hidden state norms per layer. Compare to $\|\gamma_l\|$.

\item \textbf{BOS axis.} Feed a prompt, extract hidden states per layer. For
each layer, compute $\hat{b}_l$ from position~0 and $\theta_{l,t}$ for any LM
head row $W_t$.

\item \textbf{Steering (traversal).} Forward-pass a prompt to get $h$. For
target token~$t$, compute $g_t$, step with $\alpha$, renormalize by
$\|\gamma_L\|$, then feed $h'$ as the first-generation hidden state. Verified
across 1,000 random tokens on each of 4 models (91--98\% rank-1, 100\% rank
improvement).

\item \textbf{Cow tipping.} For token~$t$, decode, re-encode, forward-pass to
get $h_T$. Compute $s(T)$. If $s(T) > 0.01$, feed $t$ three times and check
15-step self-prediction with greedy decoding. Record permanence and $\cos$
stability.
\end{enumerate}
